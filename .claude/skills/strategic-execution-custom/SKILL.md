# Strategic Problem Solving, Execution & Influence

A framework for senior individual contributors who own complex, high-impact initiatives. Use this skill to self-assess, prepare for performance conversations, identify development areas, or coach on these behaviors.

## Use Cases

- **Self-assessment**: Rate yourself against each dimension before a review cycle
- **Development planning**: Identify gaps and create targeted improvement plans
- **Coaching prep**: Structure feedback conversations around specific behaviors
- **Project retrospective**: Evaluate how well you demonstrated these competencies on a recent initiative

## Process

### Step 1: Select Mode

Ask the user:

```
How would you like to use this framework?

1. **Self-Assessment** - Rate yourself across all dimensions
2. **Development Planning** - Identify gaps and create an action plan
3. **Project Retrospective** - Evaluate a specific initiative
4. **Coaching Prep** - Prepare feedback for someone else
```

---

### Step 2A: Self-Assessment Mode

Walk through each competency area. For each, ask the user to rate themselves (1-5) and provide a specific example.

**Rating Scale:**
- 1 = Not yet demonstrating
- 2 = Emerging / inconsistent
- 3 = Solid / meets expectations
- 4 = Strong / role model
- 5 = Exceptional / sets the bar

#### Problem Solving & Strategic Thinking

| Competency | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| Portfolio ownership | Independently owns 3-4 complex, high-impact initiatives with material business impact |
| Problem framing | Defines ambiguous problem spaces, frames the right questions, develops clear and testable hypotheses |
| Strategic depth | Evaluates trade-offs, risks, and downstream implications of strategic choices |
| Prioritization | Prioritizes based on business impact, feasibility, and strategic alignment |
| Leadership alignment | Secures buy-in on highest-leverage bets before committing significant resources |

For each competency, ask:
- "How would you rate yourself on [competency]? (1-5)"
- "What's a specific recent example that demonstrates this?"

After all ratings, summarize:
- Strengths (ratings of 4-5)
- Development areas (ratings of 1-2)
- Ask: "Which 1-2 areas do you want to focus on developing?"

---

### Step 2B: Development Planning Mode

1. Ask which competency areas feel weakest or most important to develop
2. For each area, explore:
   - What's getting in the way?
   - What would "good" look like in your current context?
   - What's one concrete action you could take in the next 2 weeks?
3. Create a simple development plan with:
   - Focus area
   - Specific behavior to practice
   - Upcoming opportunity to practice it
   - How you'll know you're improving

---

### Step 2C: Project Retrospective Mode

1. Ask the user to describe a recent initiative
2. Walk through each competency area:
   - "How well did you demonstrate [competency] on this project?"
   - "What would you do differently?"
3. Identify patterns across competencies
4. Extract 2-3 concrete learnings to carry forward

---

### Step 2D: Coaching Prep Mode

1. Ask who they're preparing to coach and context
2. For each competency area, ask:
   - "Where do they excel?"
   - "Where do they need development?"
3. Help craft specific, behavioral feedback with examples
4. Suggest development actions they could recommend

---

- **Development plan**: Save to `03-Tasks/Tasks.md` or quarterly goals
- **Retrospective**: Save to the project page or learnings

## The Full Framework Reference

### Problem Solving & Strategic Thinking
- Independently owns a portfolio of 3–4 complex, high-impact initiatives with material business impact.
- Defines ambiguous problem spaces, frames the right questions, and develops clear, compelling, and testable hypotheses.
- Demonstrates depth of thinking by evaluating trade-offs, risks, and downstream implications of strategic choices.
- Prioritizes work based on business impact, feasibility, and strategic alignment.
- Secures leadership alignment and buy-in on the highest-leverage bets before committing significant time and resources.

### Execution & Process Ownership
- Operates with strong ownership across a portfolio of initiatives, not just individual projects.
- Applies a structured and repeatable approach to driving work forward, including:
  - Identifying when deep analytics is required versus directional insight.
  - Recognizing valid business problems where existing solutions are insufficient and require reframing.
  - Distinguishing between initiatives that need escalation, alignment, iteration, or ideation.
- Drives initiatives end-to-end, moving work effectively through:
  - Problem definition and ideation
  - Business casing and impact sizing
  - Solution design and validation
  - RTP definition
  - Launch and post-launch iteration
- Proactively seeks input and thought partnership when appropriate, while maintaining accountability for outcomes.

### Executive Communication & Influence
- Communicates clearly, concisely, and confidently with senior and executive stakeholders.
- Grounds recommendations in rigorous thinking, clear hypotheses, and evidence-based reasoning.
- Articulates trade-offs and decision rationale in a way that enables leadership decision-making.
- Builds sustained executive confidence by consistently demonstrating focus on the most impactful work.

### Cross-Functional Leadership & Collaboration
- Leads through influence across cross-functional partners to unlock meaningful, measurable business outcomes.
- Prioritizes business impact over consensus-building for its own sake.
- Asks thoughtful, sometimes uncomfortable questions to challenge assumptions and improve outcomes.
- Builds long-term credibility and trust by elevating the quality of thinking and results across partners.

### Scope & Impact Expectations
- Impact extends beyond individual projects to shaping direction across a problem space or portfolio.
- Operates effectively in environments with high ambiguity and multiple stakeholders.
- Contributions are visible, repeatable, and scalable, setting a bar for others to follow.
